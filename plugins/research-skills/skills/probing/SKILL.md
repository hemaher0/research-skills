# Probing

Develop a provisional hypothesis from research cues. A hypothesis may concern
a relationship, possible effect, explanatory mechanism, or partial pattern;
it need not begin as a complete causal theory or numerical prediction.

## Connect observation to conjecture

State what has actually been observed and under which conditions. Connect the
candidate hypothesis to those cues and expose the assumptions needed to get
from the observation to the claim. Identify what is reused from prior work,
what the user has contributed, and what remains an agent-proposed explanation.

Make the hypothesis specific enough to investigate: what objects or conditions
it concerns, what relation it proposes, and which cues it does or does not
explain. A partial explanatory fragment is valid. If the question itself is
unclear, identify the observation needed to formulate it more precisely.

## Work through consequences and alternatives

Derive qualitative or quantitative consequences only where the current idea
supports them. Consider another explanation when it could produce the same
observations, including measurement or implementation effects. Separate
assumptions shared by the alternatives from those that differ.

Choose an observation, analysis, comparison, or intervention that could develop
the hypothesis or reveal a difference. Explain why it is informative, which
conditions need to hold, and what it cannot settle. An observation may uncover
a new phenomenon before alternatives can be distinguished. Do not invent a
numerical range, opposing hypothesis, or deliberately failing test just to
complete a template.

Where a directional expectation or decision criterion is justified, record it
before the observation. State possible disagreement and unresolved outcomes.
Where it is not justified, name the uncertainty and what the inquiry is intended
to reveal. A convenient small test cannot replace representative evidence for
a scale-dependent hypothesis.

## Develop the hypothesis with evidence

Check evidence validity and relevance before changing the claim. Accept
surprising evidence as a reason to examine the explanation and its conditions;
familiarity with a prior theory is not grounds to reject sound observations.
Use [interpretation](../interpretation/SKILL.md) when result analysis is needed.

Retain the previous hypothesis and the evidence that motivated refinement,
combination, narrowing, or rejection. Distinguish an inspiring source from a
parent hypothesis whose content is inherited. An inconclusive observation may
leave the judgment unchanged, with a reason. Use qualitative uncertainty unless
a justified probabilistic model supports numerical confidence.

Return the working hypothesis, linked cues, added assumptions, unexplained
parts, and the next useful observation. Open, competing, revised, or shelved
hypotheses are legitimate states; no terminal verdict is compulsory. Use
[evidence-based-research](../evidence-based-research/SKILL.md) for iterative progress and
[pilot](../pilot/SKILL.md) for representative new-evidence execution when
requested. Hypothesis development alone does not authorize those runs or
require a durable registry.

## Research basis

[HEP (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09195) tracks hypothesis lineage and
evidence-linked judgment in three materials-science tasks. Its beliefs are
self-assigned and evidence validation is agent-assessed; this skill does not
adopt its fixed probability thresholds or mandatory terminal resolutions.
[Co-Scientist (Nature, 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10644-y)
uses ongoing scientist input to generate and refine hypotheses, with empirical
validation in three biomedical applications.
