# Reinterpretation

Reorganize research cues into a different way of seeing the problem. Cues may
come from results, precedents, failed reproductions, implementation behavior,
derivations, or the user's observations. An unfinished connection can be a
useful outcome.

## Make the cues comparable

For each consequential cue, retain its source, object or phenomenon, relation,
conditions, and evidence status. Distinguish a direct observation from an
interpretation or analogy. Resolve differences in definitions, scale, units,
and conditions before treating two cues as equivalent. Keep unresolved
differences visible rather than smoothing them away.

Use the user's proposed connection or unusual observation as a candidate
organizing principle. Explain which cues it connects and which it leaves
unexplained. Domain knowledge supplied during the discussion can change the
representation and the question, not merely endorse an agent's proposal.

## Change the view deliberately

Try a representation that could expose a hidden relation: group cues by
conditions or trade-offs, align input/output roles, compare dependency
structures, change the unit of analysis, or view exceptions as a separate
regime. Explain what this view makes visible and what information it suppresses.
Use a diagram, mathematical representation, or transformed measurements only
when it helps the particular connection.

Look for shared structure, boundary conditions, complementary failures, or an
assumption that the current framing hides. Recombination may suggest a different
question or measurement target as well as a method. Method combinations alone
do not require inventing a new scientific question.

When borrowing an idea across domains, specify the correspondence between
objects and relations, the restrictions under which it could hold, and where
it breaks. Surface resemblance or similar vocabulary is insufficient evidence
for equivalence or a common causal mechanism.

## Preserve the useful uncertainty

Describe the candidate connection, how it changes the current understanding,
the cues that support it, and the gaps or counterexamples that weaken it.
Keep competing framings when the evidence cannot choose among them. Check the
closest precedents with [exploration](../exploration/SKILL.md) when available
and a novelty or cross-domain claim matters; without it, inspect primary
sources directly, compare definitions/conditions, and state search limits; an appealing framing is not itself proof of novelty.

Use [probing](../probing/SKILL.md) when available to develop a provisional
explanation, and [evidence-based-research](../evidence-based-research/SKILL.md)
when available to choose an informative attempt. Without them, state the
connection's assumptions, derive supported consequences, compare alternatives
and name the observation that could distinguish them directly. Transforming results to answer the existing
measurement question belongs to [interpretation](../interpretation/SKILL.md);
this skill applies when the relationship, concept, or question is reframed.
Do not require a settled theory, polished proposal, or separate document.

## Research basis

[HALO (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18564) studies grouping and recombining
candidate strategies with medicinal chemists; its user-study evidence is
domain-specific. [Abduction Without a Body (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02505)
proposes representation-based structural correspondences; its motivating case
and evaluation proposal do not establish general discovery capability.
